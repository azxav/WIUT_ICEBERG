Traffic events from a fixed camera

Team Iceberg, WIUT Hackathon 2026, computer vision track.

Back to the site

Problem

Given several minutes of 25 fps video from a fixed road camera, output every traffic event of fourteen classes as [start, end, label] (Part A, macro F1 at tIoU 0.3, 0.5 and 0.7), and a causal per-frame probability that an accident starts within five seconds (Part B). Everything runs offline on one T4 within three times the video length.

Method

  • Perception, once per video. YOLO11m on every 2nd frame, ByteTrack, then stitching of broken tracks and smoothed kinematics (speed in box heights per second, a cheap perspective correction).
  • Scene. Lanes with direction, stop lines, solid lines, crossings and signal lamps drawn once per camera; signal state from lamp pixel colour; a flow field of the usual travel direction learned from the video's own tracks.
  • Rules. One rule per class on trajectories and geometry (for example: front crosses a stop line while its lamp is red). Segments are cleaned with hysteresis, merging, minimum length and per-class boundary offsets.
  • Risk. A causal model over the frame stream scores the chance of a collision in the next five seconds.

Why this design

  • A few labelled samples cannot train a fourteen-class video model; most classes are geometric and rules state them exactly.
  • Rules give sharp boundaries, which the 0.7 tIoU threshold rewards.
  • Rules re-run from cached tracks in seconds, so every threshold is tuned against our labels.
  • A wrongly emitted absent class costs a whole zero-F1 class, so rare classes run with strict thresholds.

Limits and next steps

  • Scene layouts are hand-drawn per camera; a new camera needs ten minutes of annotation before the geometric rules apply.
  • Objects outside COCO (debris, boxes) are missed by the detector, which limits road obstacle recall.
  • Night glare and saturated lamps shift signal-state changes, costing boundary precision on red-light events.
  • The samples are small: our scores carry wide uncertainty, and thresholds may overfit them.
